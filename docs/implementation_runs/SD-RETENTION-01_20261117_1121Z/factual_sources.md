## Authoritative Technical & Regulatory Sources
1. **14 CFR § 25.775 — Windshields and windows** (Electronic Code of Federal Regulations / FAA):
   - Establishes structural pressure-differential design requirements and fail-safe / single-failure safety criteria so no single structural failure of a transparent pane leads to sudden cabin depressurization.
2. **FAA Advisory Circular AC 25.775-1 — Window and Windshield Design, Certification, and Maintenance**:
   - Documents multipane fail-safe certification principles in which a primary pressure-bearing pane carries operational cabin pressure load while a backup structural pane must withstand fail-safe loads if the primary pane fails.
3. **Commercial Multi-Pane Cabin Window Architecture (Boeing / PPG Aerospace / GKN Aerospace public engineering explanations)**:
   - Modern pressurized passenger cabin windows typically comprise **3 transparent panes**:
     - **Outer structural pane**: Sealed to the fuselage frame; bears the primary cabin-to-outside air pressure differential during flight.
     - **Middle fail-safe pane**: Contains the small **bleed / breather hole** near the bottom edge. Because air passes slowly through that tiny opening, cabin pressure acts uniformly against the outer pane rather than loading the middle pane during normal flight. If the outer pane were ever compromised, the middle pane takes the load while air escapes only gradually through the small orifice.
     - **Inner scratch / reveal pane**: Non-structural interior plastic cover protecting the middle and outer panes from passenger scratches and smudges.
   - **Moisture & Fog Prevention**: The bleed hole also allows warm, humid cabin air and trapped inter-pane moisture to equilibrate and vent gradually so frost and condensation do not obscure the window during high-altitude cold exposure.
