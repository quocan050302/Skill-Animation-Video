# Factual Sources — SD-RETENTION-04.R2 (2026-11-18)

## Demo A: London Great Smog (Dec 5–9, 1952)
1. **UK Met Office — The Great Smog of 1952**: Temperature inversion trapping domestic hearth & coal power plant sulfur/soot aerosols over London Dec 5–9, 1952; zero-visibility transport disruption.
2. **London City Hall / Museum of London Archives**: Estimated ~4,000 short-term excess deaths during/immediately after December 1952 (`~4,000 EST.`).
3. **UK Clean Air Act 1956**: Legislative response promoting smokeless heating zones and fuels.

## Demo B: Mount St. Helens Eruption (May 18, 1980)
1. **USGS Fact Sheet 036-00** (`https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2000/fs036-00/`): May 18, 1980 at 8:32 a.m. PDT — M 5.1 earthquake triggered massive landslide on unstable north flank, uncorking pressurized system into catastrophic lateral blast devastated roughly 230–250 sq mi (~230 sq mi forestry/land zone cited in USGS summary; ~250 sq mi total blast/tephra reach in summary overview), followed by towering vertical Plinian ash column.
2. **USGS General Interest Publication — The Catastrophic Eruption** (`https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/msh/catastrophic.html`): First-minute sequence — slope failure / debris avalanche (largest subaerial landslide in recorded history) uncorked confining overburden, releasing superheated steam/gas/pulverized rock laterally before deep conduit Plinian eruption column rose tens of miles for ~9 hours.
3. **USGS National Volcanic Monument Geology** (`https://www.usgs.gov/geology-and-ecology-of-national-parks/geology-mount-st-helens-national-volcanic-monument`): Shallow intruding **cryptodome** pushed north flank outward by hundreds of feet ("bulge") from March to May 1980 prior to failure.
4. **USGS Volcanic Ash Context** (`https://volcanoes.usgs.gov/volcanic_ash/mount_st_helens_1980.html`): Fine abrasive volcanic tephra/ash dispersal across Pacific Northwest downwind states.
