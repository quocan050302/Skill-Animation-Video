# Factual & Historical Sources — "The Day London Disappeared into Smog" (Dec 1952)

- **Title**: The Day London Disappeared into Smog
- **Physics Fidelity Disclosure**: Atmospheric temperature inversion, coal combustion cutaway, and particle dispersion are stylized educational 3D visualizations (`visualized_physical_approximation`), NOT CFD or dispersion solver outputs. Upper inversion layer is labeled **`ILLUSTRATIVE AIRFLOW`** to represent atmospheric thermal stratification rather than a solid barrier.

## Primary Grounded Reference Facts
1. **Event Window (`claim_dates_dec5_9_1952`)**:
   - Dates: **December 5–9, 1952** (`"DEC 5-9, 1952"`, five days).
   - Sources: UK Met Office ("The Great Smog of 1952" archive), London Museum ("The Killer Fog of 1952").
2. **Combustion & Pollutants (`claim_coal_sulfur_smoke`)**:
   - Mechanism: Freezing December weather sharply increased household soft coal hearth burning alongside industrial & power-station coal combustion, discharging soot and sulfur dioxide into humid Thames basin air.
   - Sources: UK Met Office, London Museum.
3. **Meteorological Mechanism (`claim_temperature_inversion_1952`)**:
   - Mechanism: A slow-moving winter anticyclone placed warmer air aloft over colder ground air (**temperature inversion**), suppressing vertical convection and holding soot-laden fog close to street level.
   - Sources: UK Met Office historical meteorology summary.
4. **Urban Visibility Collapse (`claim_zero_visibility_Dec1952`)**:
   - Effect: Streetlights stayed lit through midday in parts of London; road traffic halted or buses crawled behind conductors carrying lamps/flares ahead on foot.
   - Sources: London Transport historical accounts / London Museum.
5. **Estimated Mortality (`claim_excess_deaths_est_4000`)**:
   - Wording (STRICTLY QUALIFIED): **`an estimated 4,000 excess deaths that month`** (contemporaneous Ministry of Health / London City Hall initial estimate; card badge surfaced strictly as **`~4,000 DEATHS EST.`**).
   - Sources: London City Hall / UK Public Health historical archive.
6. **Policy & Long-Term Outcome (`claim_clean_air_act_1956`)**:
   - Law: **Clean Air Act 1956** (gradually introducing smoke-control areas and smokeless heating fuels over subsequent years — never depicted as instant clearing).
   - Sources: UK Parliament / UK Environment Legislation records.
