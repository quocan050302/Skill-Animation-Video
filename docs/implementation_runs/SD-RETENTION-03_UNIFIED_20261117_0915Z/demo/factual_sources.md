# Factual Sources Note — *The Bridge That Twisted Itself Apart* (Tacoma Narrows, 1940)

All quantitative values, dates, and engineering causes depicted in this stylized illustrative short are cross-checked against public engineering records:

1. **Washington State Department of Transportation (WSDOT) — Tacoma Narrows Bridge History**:
   - Opening date: **July 1, 1940** across The Narrows of Puget Sound, Washington State.
   - Nickname: **Galloping Gertie** due to persistent vertical deck oscillations in moderate winds.
   - Dimensions: **2,800-foot** center main span, **5,939-foot** total bridge length, **39-foot** deck width, **8-foot** solid plate stiffening girders.
   - Collapse timeline on **November 7, 1940**: transition from vertical bounce to torsional twisting around **~10:03 a.m.**, center ~**600-foot** section collapsing into Puget Sound around **~11:02 a.m.**
   - Human impact: **Zero human fatalities** during the collapse.

2. **U.S. Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) / Aerospace & Structural Post-Collapse Reports**:
   - Mechanism clarified as **aeroelastic torsional flutter** driven by solid plate girders interacting with crosswinds rather than pure forced vortex resonance alone.
   - Direct engineering outcome: adoption of deep open truss stiffening girders and aerodynamic wind-tunnel testing for long-span suspension bridges worldwide (including the 1950 replacement Tacoma Narrows span).
